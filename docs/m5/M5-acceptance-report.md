The workspace this was written in cannot reach Google's Maven repository, emulators or Actions logs, so everything Android ran in GitHub Actions; the session state machine and the notification mapping, with their 56 tests, were also compiled and run locally against stubs, ktlint ran locally on every Kotlin change, and the secrets scan on the final tree; CI runs both on every push as well. CI reports test results, the end-to-end evidence, the VPN lifecycle and every failure as annotations readable through the GitHub API.

The owner delegated the networking decisions ("Timi nai decide gara"); ADR-0010 was accepted on that basis. The pre-flight target (`connectivitycheck.gstatic.com:443`) is an engineering default inside open decision **D6** (what "Test connection" contacts, and what the privacy policy names); **D2** (plaintext proxies in the Play build) and **D7** (TLS default in the form) stay open from M4. None of them blocks M6.

What goes through the VPN is unchanged from M1 and M4 and stated in the traffic support matrix: only TCP (IPv4 and IPv6) goes through the proxy, by name where the app asked by name; DNS is answered on the phone with fake addresses so the proxy resolves names; UDP other than DNS is refused; ICMP is never answered. M5 proves the TCP path on four Android versions with real proxy servers and packet captures; M6 measures DNS, TLS-to-proxy and the refused traffic on devices.

## Acceptance criteria

| Criterion (brief) | Evidence | Result |
|---|---|---|
| VPN permission is requested clearly and before tunnel activation | Connect calls `VpnService.prepare()` first; when it returns an intent, Home shows the explainer B9 ("Allow a VPN connection", what the VPN does and that only one VPN app can be active) and only Continue opens Android's dialog; a refusal or back-press shows H1 and starts nothing (`HomeViewModelTest`, `HomeScreenTest.theExplainerComesBeforeAndroidsConsentScreen`, `aRefusedPermissionShowsH1`). The session checks consent again before every attempt and ends with `VPN_PERMISSION_DENIED` without it (`withoutConsentNothingStarts`, `anInterfaceAndroidRefusesEndsWithPermissionDenied`, `consentGoneDuringASwitchEndsTheConnection`). On the emulators, through the real screens and Android's own dialog (`com.android.vpndialogs`): `VpnConsentTest.refusingAndroidsDialogShowsWhyAndChangesNothing` (explainer, dialog, Cancel → H1; no VPN network, no interface established, no service) and `allowingAndroidsDialogConnectsThroughTheProxy` (OK → Connected, traffic through Squid, Disconnect releases everything); while the dialog is open nothing has started | **Pass** |
| Supported test TCP traffic reaches the destination through the configured proxy | `socks5TrafficReachesTheDestinationThroughTheProxyByName` and `httpConnectTrafficReachesTheDestinationThroughTheProxyByName`: a shell command (the shell is covered by the VPN; ProxyFlow itself is excluded) sends a line to `proxyflow.test:8080`, a name only the proxies can resolve, and gets the destination's answer back; Connected is checked as Android sees it (one interface, Android's VPN network leading into it and VALIDATED, the engine running). Lines also go through after a switch, after Restart, in the consent test and in the listener test. Afterwards `scripts/m5_evidence.py` matches every line the tests logged against the destination's log (each arrived exactly once), the destination's connections against the tunnels Squid and Dante opened (equal: nothing arrived around a proxy), checks that Squid was asked for the destination by name, and reads packet captures of the phone's uplink: SYNs to both proxies, no packet to or from the destination's address, no DNS query for its name. In all three runs and on all four API levels: 8 lines exchanged, each arrived once; the destination's 10 connections all came through the proxies (4 Squid tunnels asked by name, 6 Dante tunnels); nothing reached the sink; no capture of the uplink has a packet to the destination's address or a DNS query for its name (End-to-end evidence below). | **Pass** |
| Invalid proxy never displays Connected | The pre-flight check runs before any interface exists and passes only when the proxy opens a tunnel to `connectivitycheck.gstatic.com:443` (`everyAnswerButAnOpenedTunnelFailsTheCheck`: every proxy-level, refusal, general-failure, unreachable and unsupported answer fails it). On the emulators wrong passwords for Dante and Squid (`AUTH_FAILED`), a closed port (`PROXY_UNREACHABLE`) and a silent proxy (cancelled, and `HANDSHAKE_TIMEOUT` in the switch test) never show Connected, never establish an interface and never give Android a VPN network (`aWrongPasswordNeverConnects`, `anUnreachableProxyNeverConnects`, `cancellingTheCheckChangesNothing`, `VpnConsentTest.aWrongPasswordSaysWhyAndNeverShowsConnected` on the real screens). Even a passing check is not Connected yet: the session waits until Android routes apps into the interface (`connectedWaitsUntilAndroidRoutesAppsIntoTheInterface`, `aFreshInterfaceAndroidNeverRoutesIsTakenDownAgain`). These tests record every state the controller published and assert Connected never appeared | **Pass** |
| Disconnect and permission revocation close TUN, sockets and worker jobs | Every exit stops the engine first, then closes the interface, then the service (`disconnectStopsTheEngineBeforeClosingTheInterfaceThenTheService`, `revocationReleasesEverythingAndSaysSo`, `aDestroyedServiceReleasesWhatIsLeft`). On the emulators each test ends with `assertReleased`: no `/dev/tun` descriptor in the process, no service instance, the engine stopped, no session job left, no VPN network in Android, the status notification gone. `revocationClosesEverythingAndSaysSo` withdraws the permission with `appops` and lets Android revoke: `VPN_REVOKED`, service stopped, interface closed, network gone, the alert shown, and a new connection clears it. A cancelled check is cut short instead of running into its 15 s timeout (`aCancelledCheckIsCutShortAndLeavesNoJobBehind`, `aCancelledCheckIsCutShortEvenOnASingleThread`; on the emulators within 3 s) | **Pass** |
| Repeated connect/disconnect cycles do not leak resources or create duplicate services | `repeatedConnectAndDisconnectLeaveNothingBehind`: ten cycles through Dante, each with one interface and one service while connected and everything released after; afterwards the process's sockets are back to the count after the second cycle and its threads within 6 of it. `repeatedStartsMakeOneSession`: five `connect()` calls and an extra start of the service itself make one interface, one service and one Connected. JVM: `aSecondConnectForTheSameProfileIsIgnored`, `repeatedCyclesReleaseEveryInterfaceEngineAndService` (20 cycles) | **Pass** |
| Foreground notification accurately reflects connection state | The service goes into the foreground with the first state of an attempt and updates the same notification for each state: Connecting with its step and Cancel, Reconnecting ("traffic paused") with Disconnect, Connected with its timer and Disconnect, Blocked ("Traffic blocked") with the reason, Restart and Disconnect — only Disconnect when the connection's profile was deleted, since Restart could not work (`StatusContentTest`); revocation posts "Disconnected by Android" on the problems channel. Updates are at least 300 ms apart with the last state always posted (Android drops faster updates), and a late stop never removes a newer connection's notification. On the emulators: Connected with only Disconnect, a private notification whose lock-screen version names no profile (threat model I-11), "Traffic blocked" after an engine failure, the revocation alert, and no notification after every disconnect | **Pass** |

## The brief's tasks

| Task | Where |
|---|---|
| 1. Implement VpnService and call `VpnService.prepare()` before starting | `ProxyFlowVpnService` (`core/vpn`); `prepare()` when the user taps Connect (`HomeViewModel` → `ConnectionController.consentIntent()`) and again before every attempt (`VpnHost.hasConsent()`); ADR-0010 §2, §10 |
| 2. Build the TUN interface with documented addresses, routes and MTU | `ProxyFlowVpnService.establish()` with the engine's network plan: `198.18.0.1/32` and `fd00:66::1/128`, routes `0.0.0.0/0` and `::/0`, DNS `198.18.0.2`, MTU 9000, the app excluded, never `allowBypass`, non-blocking; ADR-0010 §3, `docs/m1/06-architecture.md` §4 |
| 3. Process TUN packets off the main thread with the selected engine | the M4 engine (gVisor netstack, Go) reads the descriptor on its own goroutines; the session's events run on a serial background dispatcher, the check and the routing wait on their own coroutines; nothing networking runs on the main thread |
| 4. Protect upstream proxy sockets with `VpnService.protect()` | every socket the engine and the pre-flight check open goes through `VpnHost.protectSocket()` before connecting, and a refused protect fails the connection (M4 `errProtect`); the app is also excluded from its own VPN |
| 5. Foreground service notification and Android-compliant lifecycle | `startForeground` with type `systemExempted` (Android 14+, ADR-0007) from the first state of an attempt, `START_NOT_STICKY`, `stopSelfResult()`, `BIND_VPN_SERVICE`, not exported; notifications in `VpnNotifications` and `StatusContent`; ADR-0010 §8, §9 |
| 6. Connect service state to UI with observable state | `ConnectionController.state: StateFlow<ConnectionState>` (status, profile and its settings, step, connected-since, error), collected by Home; ADR-0010 §1 |
| 7. Permission denial and revocation, service destruction, app disconnect, fatal engine error | H1 and `VPN_PERMISSION_DENIED`; `onRevoke()` → `VPN_REVOKED` with B6d and an alert; a destroyed service ends what is left; Disconnect from Home and the notification; `engine_failed` → Blocked (`ENGINE_STOPPED`) holding traffic until Restart or Stop; ADR-0010 §5–§8 |
| 8. Prevent duplicate starts and release resources | one session per process, one event at a time; duplicate `connect()` ignored; every path stops the engine, closes the interface, stops the service and ends the session's jobs; ADR-0010 §2, §5 |
| 9. Verify TCP through a controlled proxy with server logs and packet capture | the device tests against Dante and Squid on the CI runner, `scripts/m5_evidence.py` over the destination's and the proxies' logs, logcat and the packet captures; the evidence section below |

The destination's 10 connections are the 8 lines and the runner's own check through each proxy before the emulator starts. Squid's 5 CONNECTs for the pre-flight target are that check, the three connections the tests make through Squid (each checked first) and the wrong password, which Squid refused with 407; Dante's 2 refused sign-ins are the two wrong-password tests through it.

In every run of the verified commit: each line the tests sent reached the destination exactly once; the destination saw exactly as many connections as the proxies opened tunnels to it, so nothing reached it around a proxy; Squid was always asked for `proxyflow.test` by name, never by address (the phone did not resolve it); the sink logged no connection and no proxy was asked for it, so nothing sent while traffic was held got out, then or later; the captures of the uplink show the app's SYNs to both proxies, no packet to or from `10.99.0.1` and no DNS query for `proxyflow.test`; neither test password appears anywhere in logcat. The proxies also carried other apps' traffic (Google services) while connected, refused where Squid's rules refuse it (port 5228): all apps' TCP goes through the proxy, not only the tests'.

None of the three runs needed a workaround for the emulator: no attempt retried after an uplink drop, no not-responding dialog dismissed, and Android logged no change of the uplink during the tests.

The lifecycle log (tag `ProxyFlowVpn`, emitted as a notice per API level) shows each connection's steps: `connect`, `service created`, `check …: ok`, `established tun0`, `connected`, `disconnect`, `disconnected`, `service stop 1: stopping`, `service destroyed`; for a switch `connect … (reconnect)`, the check's result and, before the next engine, `dropped N packets held while no engine ran`.

## Decisions, deviations and open items

- **Owner decisions still open:** D2 (plaintext proxies in the Play build) and D7 (TLS default in the form), both ADR-0005, and D6 (what "Test connection" contacts). The pre-flight target `connectivitycheck.gstatic.com:443` is an engineering default inside D6: every connect opens one tunnel to it through the user's proxy (no bytes sent), the privacy policy must name it (M11), and a proxy that blocks or cannot reach that one host cannot connect.
- **What M5 does not do, by plan:** following network changes, reconnecting with back-off, the offline state and the escalation rule for a proxy that dies after connecting (M7: until then such failures are refused connection by connection, never sent around the proxy, but Home keeps showing Connected); traffic counters and the session log (M7); kill-switch guidance and Always-on lockdown detection (M8); picking another profile from Home while connected, the Quick Settings tile and onboarding (M9); "Test connection" in the form (D6, M9); MTU 9000 measured on phones (M10). `docs/m5/vpn-service-integration.md` §6.
- **Emulators, not phones.** Everything Android was proven on emulators; physical phones and vendor builds come with M10. Always-on VPN starts (Android starting the service itself) are covered by JVM tests only.
- **Traffic other than TCP** behaves as the traffic support matrix states (DNS answered on the phone, UDP refused, ICMP unanswered); M5 shows DNS for the destination never left the phone, and M6 measures the rest on devices.
- **Captures.** API 29 has only the emulator's own capture (its adbd does not survive `adb root`); API 34 and 36 have it and tcpdump on the phone's interfaces; API 35, whose uplink is Wi-Fi, has only the phone's tcpdump, since the emulator's capture does not see that image's Wi-Fi.
- **What the tests work around on the emulator, and count:** an attempt that the phone's own uplink failed ("no route to the proxy") is tried once more, and another app's "isn't responding" dialog over Android's VPN dialog is dismissed. Neither touches what the tests prove: the failed attempt still has to leave nothing behind, a second failure or any other error fails the test, and the evidence line "emulator hiccups the tests worked around" gives the numbers for each run.
- **A Lint check is suppressed** on the VPN service: `ForegroundServicePermission` would require a permission that `systemExempted` VPN apps do not need (they qualify through the `ACTIVATE_VPN` app op the consent dialog grants); the reason is in the manifest.
