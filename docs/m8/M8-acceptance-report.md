## Acceptance criteria

| Criterion | Evidence | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Engine failure does not silently direct-route supported traffic | While ProxyFlow's VPN interface exists nothing goes around the proxy: the engine dials the proxy before it accepts an app's connection and has no other way out, and every failure — the engine's packet loop ending, the proxy refusing, hanging or changing its certificate, a reconnection, no network, a switch — keeps the interface with no engine reading it, so apps' packets are held and dropped (ADR-0004, ADR-0010 §5, ADR-0012 §6). Verified on the emulators with packet captures since M5 — `VpnEndToEndTest.anEngineFailureHoldsTrafficUntilRestart` (the sink beside the destination gets nothing, `m5_evidence.py` finds no packet to it in the captures), M6's refusing and hanging proxies, M7's reconnections — and again in the verified run. M8 closes the one gap left at a start: an Always-on VPN start used to check the proxy before creating the interface, so apps used the normal network during the check; now the interface comes first and a failing proxy is retried with traffic held (`KillSwitchTest`: the interface before the check, a proxy away retried with traffic held, a refused sign-in blocked, an unexpected failure blocked; scenario B on the emulators starts ProxyFlow this way). ## Reviews and what they changed

Two independent reviews read the work, each told to find defects rather than confirm: the app, the session and the engine (`f339a75..ee0848f`); and the failure injection, its evidence and the build's checks. Five more reviewers then read the fixes in turn, each the previous round's, the last one the APK read-back alone. Every finding was fixed or, where noted, documented as a limitation. The reviewers rated their findings medium at most, on their own scales; by the security review's (§1), a claim the app makes that is false is high, and the first review's two mediums were that — on-screen statements that could be false — as were the lines, found then and in later rounds, that stated an old reading of Android's kill switch as current, rated low or as nits. They are the security review's F12, high and fixed. No other finding was critical or high, and none is open.

**The app, the session and the engine**

| Finding | Fix |
|---|---|

| Medium (high by §1, F12): an Always-on VPN start's first check looked like a user's connect — "your phone's network is unchanged" while apps' traffic was held, and a Cancel that sent apps to the normal network without asking or the tap guard | the state says traffic is held (`held`): Home's B3b says so and offers Stop, which asks first with the guard; the notification says so and offers Disconnect (`StatusContentTest.anAlwaysOnStartHoldingTrafficOffersDisconnectNotCancel`) |

## Decisions, deviations and open items

- **ADR-0013 accepted under the owner's delegation** ("Timi nai decide gara"): two kill switches and no toggle; what Android says shown as it said it, with its time; an Always-on VPN start that holds traffic first and connects only the chosen profile; a process that ended while connected found and explained, never reconnected of ProxyFlow's own accord; the SOCKS5 unanswered rule; sanitized crash reports and `ExceptionSecret`; the tap guard on the actions that let traffic out or delete data; secure defaults written out and checked in the APK; the bounds and their tests; OSV in CI. It can be reopened.
- **Owner decisions still open:** D2 (plaintext proxies in the Play build) and D7 (TLS default in the form), both ADR-0005; D6 (what "Test connection" contacts, and the pre-flight target the privacy policy must name). None blocks M9.
- **A dependency changed in M8:** DataStore 1.1.7 → 1.3.0 (stable since 2026-10-21), for the race above. The release runtime classpath went from 131 libraries to 130: DataStore's twelve at 1.3.0, with androidx.annotation 1.10.0, collection 1.6.0, tracing 2.0.1 (and tracing-android), okio 3.17.0 and atomicfu 0.28.0 coming with them, and four old Kotlin runtime artifacts gone (kotlin-android-extensions-runtime and kotlin-parcelize-runtime 1.9.22, kotlin-stdlib-jdk7 and -jdk8 1.8.0); the newcomers are Apache-2.0 like the rest (DataStore's repackaged protobuf stays BSD-3-Clause, M2). OSV checks them like every other library. The protection store keeps reading again after its own writes, so what Home shows of an interruption does not rest on the upstream fix alone.
- **What ProxyFlow can't prevent, said where it matters (R1):** without Android's kill switch, apps use the normal network once the VPN goes away — the user's disconnect, revocation, the process ending. M8 measures it (scenario A), tells the user afterwards (B2's interruption notice), and guides to the only setting that keeps apps off the network then (E2).
- **Android did not start ProxyFlow again** after its process was killed with Always-on VPN and blocking on, within the probes (26 to 31 s, on every level and attempt of the verified run): with blocking on, apps then have no network until the user opens ProxyFlow and connects (R4). E2 and the limitations say so; ProxyFlow does not reconnect of its own accord on its next start (ADR-0013 §5).
- **What the failure injection covers:** the shell's TCP to one IPv4 address (the witness), Android's VPN network list, the captures of the uplink and Android's crash report. UDP, DNS and IPv6 from apps while ProxyFlow is gone are not probed; the captures would show them on the uplink, and M5 and M6 check the whole run's DNS and captures for the lab's names and addresses. Android's kill switch is set through a device owner (`DevicePolicyManager.setAlwaysOnVpnPackage(…, lockdown = true)`), the setting Android's VPN settings make, reached another way — turning it off that way withdrew ProxyFlow's consent on Android 14, 15 and 16 (not on Android 10), which the user's own settings are not known to do. On emulators, not phones: physical phones and vendor builds come with M10.
- **Android writes an uncaught exception's message to logcat itself** before any app handler runs (R7): scenario C counts that line. Logcat is readable by ProxyFlow and adb, not by other apps. ProxyFlow's own messages hold no secrets (`ExceptionSecret`); what could appear there is a library's message.
- **The kill switch's status while disconnected is what Android said when ProxyFlow last ran (R8)**, shown with its time; the user may have changed it since.
- **Limitations of the hardening itself, documented:** the tap guard's message is a toast, and on Android 10 and 11 whether ProxyFlow's own toast makes the next taps count as covered is not verified (the message comes at most every 3.5 s); `ExceptionSecret` looks at the expressions in an exception's message, not values passed through locals or parameters, nor an overridden `message` (the crash handler sanitizes whatever reaches it); the marker is removed when Android detaches the VPN service, which it does only on the way to the session's end.
- **For M9 and later, by plan:** the Quick Settings tile (exported only behind `BIND_QUICK_SETTINGS_TILE`, M9); physical phones, memory and battery on low-end devices (M10); Play App Signing and the signed release build (M11).
