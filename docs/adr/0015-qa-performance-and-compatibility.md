# ADR-0015 — QA, performance and compatibility (M10)

**Status:** Accepted 2026-11-04 (technical decisions delegated by the owner, "Timi nai decide gara"; implemented in M10) · **Date:** 2026-11-04

## Context

M10 asks to prove reliability and routing correctness before release: every kind of test run; valid and invalid HTTP and SOCKS5 proxies, failed sign-ins and timeouts; DNS, TLS, IPv4 and IPv6, Wi-Fi and mobile switching, captive portals and proxy failure; permission revocation, process death, reboot and battery optimization; packet captures and controlled endpoints for direct leaks; CPU, memory, battery, throughput and latency; representative devices and every declared Android version; accessibility and localization; defects filed, prioritised, resolved and regression-tested. Its acceptance: release-blocking tests pass, no direct bypass in the supported-TCP test matrix, no critical or high security defect, performance recorded against agreed targets (the V1 quality bars, `docs/m1/01-scope.md` §7), a complete device and OS matrix, and known limitations documented and said in the app.

What M5–M9 left: device tests on four Android versions (API 29, 34, 35, 36) of the eight the app supports (minSdk 29 to targetSdk 36); no performance measurement since the M1 spike's Linux VM; no test of a captive portal, of Android's power saving, of the notification permission denied, or of a restart; English only, never seen in a longer language or right to left; and every run on emulators — M1's condition that the lab checks E01 and E07–E10 run on a physical phone with packet capture is still open (`docs/m1/09-feasibility-report.md` §7).

## Decision

1. **Every declared Android version runs the whole device suite.** CI's device matrix is API 29 to 36 (Android 10 to 16), each a `google_apis` x86_64 emulator with the M5–M8 lab, captures and evidence; the four levels M5 added are joined by 30–33. Physical phones are the owner's: `docs/m10/physical-phone-checklist.md` runs E01, E07–E10, battery, throughput and what only a real network shows (a sign-in page, a real Wi-Fi to mobile move) on their phone with their proxy, capturing at a laptop's hotspot. Representative devices beyond that are M11's closed testing, whose crash and ANR reports give the crash-free bar.
2. **Performance is measured on each emulator and set against the bars, never mistaken for them.** `PerformanceEndToEndTest` measures what the bars name — time to Connected (p50/p90 of 20 per protocol), a connection's set-up, a dead proxy's connections refused and none opened, throughput each way (median of three) with ProxyFlow's CPU, memory with 200 connections open, CPU idle with the screen off — and, beside each figure the emulator limits, the same without ProxyFlow: a TCP connection straight to the proxy, the same transfer straight through it from the app's own process. `scripts/m10_performance.py` reports them per level: a figure past its bar on an emulator is a warning, since an emulator's CPU is the runner's, shared with the lab, and its network the emulator's own; a missing measurement or a connection opened through a refusing proxy fails the run. The engine's stats add the Go runtime's own memory (heap, stacks, what it holds from Android), the engine's share of the app process's resident memory, which is what the memory bar is about. M1's condition 4 (switch to the hev engine if devices miss the bars) is judged on the owner's phone; the emulators give no reason to (`docs/m10/performance-baseline.md`).
3. **A network with a sign-in page is said, and signing in is acted on (defect M10-1).** ProxyFlow's network monitor reports whether Android found a sign-in page (a captive portal) on the phone's network — `NET_CAPABILITY_CAPTIVE_PORTAL`, on the same network as it appears and goes. Every connection state carries it (`ConnectionState.networkSignIn`), whatever the status, since it explains a failure to reach the proxy. Home shows "This network asks you to sign in" beside a failed connect, a reconnection or a block, with what to do next in each; Help has it. Signing in on the same network, while traffic is held for a retry (reconnecting, or blocked after the last attempt with a failure that may pass by itself), runs the next attempt at once, as a network that is back (`ReconnectReason.NetworkBack`) — a sign-in or certificate failure, which no retry changes, stays blocked (ADR-0012 §6). Nothing else changes: the network is no other network, so no connection is reset, and ProxyFlow never opens the sign-in page itself or sends anything around the proxy for it. Whether Android's own sign-in page loads while ProxyFlow holds traffic is Android's (its sign-in app may use the network outside VPNs, and "Block connections without VPN" may stop it), so the words say to stop ProxyFlow first if it doesn't open.
4. **Power saving is Android's, and ProxyFlow's foreground service holds through it.** Deep Doze, the most restricted App Standby bucket and Battery Saver are each turned on while connected (on battery, as Android does) and the connection must stay and carry a line through the proxy (`PowerSavingEndToEndTest`). ProxyFlow does not ask to be exempt from battery optimization: its foreground service is what keeps it on the network, and Google Play allows that request only for the uses its policy lists. Phones that stop background apps more than Android does are a known limitation: Help says that unrestricted battery use can help.
5. **No notification permission is needed to connect.** On Android 13+ without it (onboarding's "Not now"), the VPN's foreground service runs, its notice in Android's Task Manager instead of the drawer, and the connection works the same (`NotificationPermissionEndToEndTest`, run alone after the permission is revoked, since revoking ends the app's process).
6. **After a restart, ProxyFlow connects only as Android's Always-on VPN.** It has no start-at-boot of its own (Home says so since M8); with Always-on VPN and "Block connections without VPN", the phone's restart is tested end to end: Android starts ProxyFlow, which connects the chosen profile by itself, and until then nothing gets out (`scripts/m10_scenarios.sh`, scenario R).
7. **English only until D8, checked for the languages to come.** ProxyFlow declares `supportsRtl`, so a phone set to a right-to-left language mirrors its screens even with English words: six screens are screenshot tests laid out right to left (Robolectric's `ldrtl`), with the accessibility checks, and directional icons mirror (defect M10-2). Longer words are M9's 200 % font tests' (twelve screens on a 320 dp phone), and no screen holds a hard-coded word (only the design system's previews do, which ship to no user). Pseudo-locales were tried and dropped: the build's resource compiler could not pseudo-localize the merged resources (M10-6). Units of data and time stay SI symbols until the translation milestone (M10-5).
8. **The release candidate is the verified commit's release build**: the R8-minified APK and, new in M10, the App Bundle Google Play takes, from the same build, both unsigned until M11 configures signing; and, for the owner's phone checks, the same APK signed with a key CI makes for that run alone and then deletes (Android installs no unsigned APK), never for distribution.

## Alternatives considered

- **Only the four API levels of M5–M9, with 30–33 left to closed testing**: "all declared supported Android versions" is M10's own task 7, and an emulator per level costs CI time, not certainty.
- **Fail CI on a figure past its bar**: the emulators' own network caps throughput (API 34's Wi-Fi carries about 60 Mbit/s straight through the proxy too), so the bar would fail for the emulator, not for ProxyFlow; the baseline catches regressions instead, against the same path without ProxyFlow.
- **A direct baseline from the shell to the lab's destinations**: it would be a connection around the proxy in the very captures that must show none; the baselines go only to the proxy, as ProxyFlow does.
- **Detect a sign-in page by probing it ourselves**: Android already probes, and a probe of ProxyFlow's own would be a connection outside the proxy.
- **Open Android's sign-in page from Home**: Android offers it in its notification, and starting another app's sign-in flow from ProxyFlow adds nothing the notification lacks.
- **Request the battery-optimization exemption**: the foreground service already keeps ProxyFlow on the network in every power-saving mode tested, and Play allows the request only for the uses its policy lists.
- **Simulate a captive portal on the emulators by pointing Android's network checks at the lab**: Android reads those URLs when a network appears, so it would need the uplink dropped and brought back during the run — what the device script avoids (M10-4); the session's handling is tested with the network it is told of, as M7's network changes are, and the monitor's reading of Android's flag by unit test and against Android on every level.

## Consequences

- The device job runs about twice as long per level (the performance test, the power-saving tests and the scenarios after M8's); the step and job limits are raised to fit.
- `ConnectionState` gains one field, published with every state; its equality changes only when Android's view of the network does.
- The physical-phone checklist is the owner's; until it is filled in, M1's condition 1 stays open and the quality bars' phone figures are unmeasured, which the M10 report says.
