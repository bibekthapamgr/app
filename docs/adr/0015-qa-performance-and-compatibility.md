# ADR-0015 — QA, performance and compatibility (M10)

**Status:** Accepted 2026-11-04 (technical decisions delegated by the owner, "Timi nai decide gara"; implemented in M10) · **Date:** 2026-11-04

## Context

M10 asks to prove reliability and routing correctness before release: every kind of test run; valid and invalid HTTP and SOCKS5 proxies, failed sign-ins and timeouts; DNS, TLS, IPv4 and IPv6, Wi-Fi and mobile switching, captive portals and proxy failure; permission revocation, process death, reboot and battery optimization; packet captures and controlled endpoints for direct leaks; CPU, memory, battery, throughput and latency; representative devices and every declared Android version; accessibility and localization; defects filed, prioritised, resolved and regression-tested. Its acceptance: release-blocking tests pass, no direct bypass in the supported-TCP test matrix, no critical or high security defect, performance recorded against agreed targets (the V1 quality bars, `docs/m1/01-scope.md` §7), a complete device and OS matrix, and known limitations documented and said in the app.

What M5–M9 left: device tests on four Android versions (API 29, 34, 35, 36) of the eight the app supports (minSdk 29 to targetSdk 36); no performance measurement since the M1 spike's Linux VM; no test of a captive portal, of Android's power saving, of the notification permission denied, or of a restart; English only, never seen in a longer language or right to left; and every run on emulators — M1's condition that the lab checks E01 and E07–E10 run on a physical phone with packet capture is still open (`docs/m1/09-feasibility-report.md` §7).

## Decision

1. **Every declared Android version runs the whole device suite.** CI's device matrix is API 29 to 36 (Android 10 to 16), each a `google_apis` x86_64 emulator with the M5–M8 lab, captures and evidence; the four levels M5 added are joined by 30–33. Physical phones are the owner's: `docs/m10/physical-phone-checklist.md` runs E01, E07–E10, battery, throughput and what only a real network shows (a sign-in page, a real Wi-Fi to mobile move) on their phone with their proxy, capturing at a laptop's hotspot. Representative devices beyond that are M11's closed testing, whose crash and ANR reports give the crash-free bar.
2. **Performance is measured on each emulator and set against the bars, never mistaken for them.** `PerformanceEndToEndTest` measures what the bars name — time to Connected (p50/p90 of 20 per protocol), a connection's set-up, a dead proxy's connections refused and none opened, throughput each way (median of three, timed without the connection's set-up, which is reported apart) with ProxyFlow's CPU per gigabyte the engine carried and an upload paced at 50 Mbit/s for the CPU there, memory with 200 connections open (idle, and with 256 KiB in flight on each — the bar is judged on that one, in MB of 10^6 bytes), CPU idle with the screen off and goroutines bounded — and, beside each figure the emulator limits, the same without ProxyFlow: a TCP connection straight to the proxy, and the same transfer straight through it from the same shell client, which then signs in to the proxy itself, the two paths alternating. Every figure is logged before the test asserts anything. `scripts/m10_performance.py` reports them per level: a figure past its bar on an emulator is a warning, since an emulator's CPU is the runner's, shared with the lab, and its network the emulator's own; a missing measurement, a figure that doesn't follow from its own inputs, or a connection opened through a refusing proxy fails the run. The engine's stats add the Go runtime's own memory (heap, stacks, what it holds from Android), the engine's share of the app process's resident memory, which is what the memory bar is about. M1's condition 4 (switch to the hev engine if devices miss the bars) is judged on the owner's phone; the emulators give no reason to (`docs/m10/performance-baseline.md`).
3. **A network with a sign-in page is said, and its going is acted on (defect M10-1, as reviewed: M10-7 to M10-15).** ProxyFlow's network monitor reports whether Android found a sign-in page (a captive portal) on the phone's network — `NET_CAPABILITY_CAPTIVE_PORTAL`, as it appears and goes on the same network; a Robolectric test checks it is Android's own flag that is read. Every connection state carries it (`ConnectionState.networkSignIn`), whatever the status, and the log notes the page and its going while ProxyFlow is on, and when a connection starts on such a network. Home shows "This network asks you to sign in" beside a failure to reach the proxy — a failed connect, a reconnection, a block — never beside one of the phone's or ProxyFlow's own (a deleted profile, no profile for Always-on, a key that can't be read, the engine stopping), with what to do in that screen's words; Help has it. The words say "may": Android's flag means only that its own check was answered by a page, and a page may let some traffic through, or answer in the proxy's place. When the page goes on the same network — the user signed in, most often — and ProxyFlow holds traffic for the proxy: while reconnecting, the attempt due next runs at once and keeps its number (one running is cut short, as it ran while the page answered), so a page that comes and goes never adds attempts; blocked with the interface up, for a failure that may pass, or for a certificate failure — the page may have answered with a certificate of its own — the attempts start again (`ReconnectReason.SignInPageGone`) without the block's detail, and the same certificate again blocks at once. A refused sign-in is the proxy's own answer and stays blocked (ADR-0012 §6). Nothing else changes: the network is no other network, so no connection is reset; a first connect or an Always-on start's first check goes on; ProxyFlow never opens the sign-in page itself nor sends anything around the proxy for it. Whether Android's own sign-in page loads while ProxyFlow holds traffic is Android's (its sign-in app may use the network outside VPNs, and "Block connections without VPN" may stop it), so the words say to stop ProxyFlow first if it doesn't open.
4. **Power saving is Android's, and ProxyFlow's foreground service holds through it.** Deep Doze (on battery, the screen off), App Standby's most restricted bucket and Battery Saver are each turned on while connected, and the connection must stay and carry a line through the proxy (`scripts/m10_scenarios.sh restart`, part P). They run while Android has started ProxyFlow as Always-on VPN, in a process of its own with no instrumentation — an instrumented process is ranked as a foreground service whatever the app does, and every power-saving mode exempts that rank, so a test inside it could not fail for ProxyFlow's sake (M10-26) — once Android's short allowance for a VPN it has just started is over; ProxyFlow's process must be ranked as its own foreground service, and Android must read back the state asked for. ProxyFlow does not ask to be exempt from battery optimization: its foreground service is what keeps it on the network, and Google Play allows that request only for the uses its policy lists. Phones that stop background apps more than Android does are a known limitation: Help says which setting can help.
5. **No notification permission is needed to connect.** On Android 13+ without it (onboarding's "Not now"), the VPN's foreground service runs, its notice in Android's Task Manager instead of the drawer, no notification of ProxyFlow's shows, and the connection works the same (`NotificationPermissionEndToEndTest`, run alone after the permission is revoked, since revoking ends the app's process: `scripts/m10_scenarios.sh notifications`, part N, while the phone's captures still run; a skipped test fails it).
6. **After a restart, ProxyFlow connects only as Android's Always-on VPN.** It has no start-at-boot of its own (Home says so since M8); with Always-on VPN and "Block connections without VPN", the phone's restart is tested end to end (`scripts/m10_scenarios.sh restart`, part R): the wait is for a new boot (a new boot id, then the boot completed), Android must still have the setting and start ProxyFlow, which connects the chosen profile by itself; until then nothing gets out — every probe of the lab's witness made while Android has no VPN network must be refused as Android's blocking refuses, none may be answered, and the report says plainly when no probe fell in that window. An Always-on start lasts until connected: a switch or a restart before then (the picker, the edit screen's reconnect) holds traffic on the same interface and is retried as the start was, rather than blocking at its first failure (M10-20).
7. **English only until D8, checked for the languages to come.** ProxyFlow declares `supportsRtl`, so a phone set to a right-to-left language mirrors its screens even with English words: six screens are screenshot tests laid out right to left (Robolectric's `ldrtl`), with the accessibility checks, and directional icons mirror (defect M10-2). Longer words are M9's 200 % font tests' (twelve screens on a 320 dp phone), and no screen holds a hard-coded word (only the design system's previews do, which ship to no user). Pseudo-locales (en-XA, ar-XB) are off: they were turned on and removed again on a wrong diagnosis of a broken build, whose cause was four strings with an apostrophe without its backslash (M10-6); they would show in a debug build what the right-to-left screenshots and the 200 % fonts already show, so they stay off. Units of data and time stay SI symbols until the translation milestone (M10-5).
8. **The release candidate is the verified commit's release build**: the R8-minified APK and, new in M10, the App Bundle Google Play takes, from the same build, both unsigned until M11 configures signing; and, for the owner's phone checks, the same APK signed with a key CI makes for that run alone and then deletes (Android installs no unsigned APK), never for distribution. The device suite runs the debug build, which R8 never touches; what R8 could break at the engine's first call is checked in the release APK itself — every class and method the engine's JNI layer finds by name must still be named in its dex files (`scripts/check_release_manifest.py`) — and the owner's phone checks run the release APK end to end (M10-18).
9. **The connection notification names no profile, and its actions need an unlocked phone (M10-17).** Disconnect sends apps to the normal network (without Android's kill switch), so it must not work from a locked phone's notification shade, nor Cancel and Restart. From Android 12 the actions carry Android's own flag (`setAuthenticationRequired`); on Android 10 and 11, which have none, they start a not-exported activity that shows nothing, which Android starts only once the phone is unlocked, and which hands the action to the service. The notification no longer names the profile: its private version, which Android shows on the lock screen when set to show all notification content, did. The privacy policy says both, and threat model I-11 holds whatever Android's setting.
10. **Every string resource is one Android's resource compiler takes**, checked in a second before any build (`scripts/check_strings.py`, M10-6): an apostrophe without its backslash broke every build of two runs, at resource merging, with a message that names no string.

## Alternatives considered

- **Only the four API levels of M5–M9, with 30–33 left to closed testing**: "all declared supported Android versions" is M10's own task 7, and an emulator per level costs CI time, not certainty.
- **Fail CI on a figure past its bar**: the emulators' own network caps throughput (API 34's Wi-Fi carries about 60 Mbit/s straight through the proxy too), so the bar would fail for the emulator, not for ProxyFlow; the baseline catches regressions instead, against the same path without ProxyFlow.
- **A direct baseline from the shell to the lab's destinations**: it would be a connection around the proxy in the very captures that must show none; the baselines go only to the proxy, as ProxyFlow does.
- **Detect a sign-in page by probing it ourselves**: Android already probes, and a probe of ProxyFlow's own would be a connection outside the proxy.
- **Open Android's sign-in page from Home**: Android offers it in its notification, and starting another app's sign-in flow from ProxyFlow adds nothing the notification lacks.
- **Request the battery-optimization exemption**: the foreground service already keeps ProxyFlow on the network in every power-saving mode tested, and Play allows the request only for the uses its policy lists.
- **Simulate a captive portal on the emulators by pointing Android's network checks at the lab**: Android reads those URLs when a network appears, so it would need the uplink dropped and brought back during the run — what the device script avoids (M10-4); the session's handling is tested with the network it is told of, as M7's network changes are; the monitor's reading of Android's flag, with Android's own classes (Robolectric); and, on every level, the monitor against Android's real report of a network without a page. A network with one is on the phone checklist.
- **Retry nothing after the page goes but what may pass**: a certificate failure blocks at once because no retry changes the proxy's own certificate, but on a network with a sign-in page the certificate may have been the page's; one attempt when the page goes accepts nothing unchecked.
- **Start the attempts over each time the page goes, as a network change does**: a page that comes and goes would then keep them going without end (M10-14).
- **Keep the profile's name in the notification's private version and say the lock screen may show it**: the name is the one thing in it the privacy policy promises the lock screen won't show; Home, the picker and the tile when unlocked still name it.
- **Send every action through the activity, on every Android version**: from Android 12 the action's own flag keeps Disconnect working in the shade, without opening anything, once unlocked; the activity only fills the versions without the flag.
- **Run the device suite against the R8 release build**: the instrumented tests reach into the app's session and runtime, which R8 renames or removes, and need a test APK built against the minified app; the names R8 could break are checked in the release APK, and the owner's phone runs it.

## Consequences

- The device job runs about twice as long per level (the performance test, and the scenarios N, P and R beside M8's); the device script keeps its own budget, each part bounded by what is left, and the step and job limits are raised to fit (130 and 155 minutes).
- `ConnectionState` gains one field, published with every state; its equality changes only when Android's view of the network does. `ReconnectReason` gains `SignInPageGone`, and the log two events (`sign_in_page`, `sign_in_page_gone`), stored and exported by their keys.
- The manifest gains one activity, not exported, that only Android 10 and 11 use; the notification shows no profile name, on any screen.
- The physical-phone checklist is the owner's; until it is filled in, M1's condition 1 stays open and the quality bars' phone figures are unmeasured, which the M10 report says.
