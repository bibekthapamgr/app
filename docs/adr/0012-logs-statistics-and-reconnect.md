# ADR-0012 — Diagnostic log, session statistics and reconnecting on its own (M7)

**Status:** Accepted 2026-11-03 (technical decisions delegated by the owner, "Timi nai decide gara"; implemented in M7) · **Date:** 2026-11-03

## Context

M7 asks for troubleshooting without collecting sensitive content: a local diagnostic event model (time, severity, event code, sanitized context); the connection's stages (starting, VPN permission, proxy handshake, authenticated, connected, reconnecting, failed, stopped); log filters, search, clear and a sanitized export; session duration and approximate upload and download bytes counted at the engine layer; bounded storage with retention and clearing; a bounded retry policy with backoff for recoverable failures, cancellable and never running twice at once; and clear, actionable errors for sign-in, timeouts, DNS, TLS and unsupported protocols. Its acceptance criteria: logs name the stage a failure happened at without secrets or payloads; storage is bounded and Clear removes the local entries; the traffic counters are checked against controlled transfers with a documented tolerance; retry attempts are limited and cancellable; and the UI stays consistent through rapid connects and disconnects and network changes.

M1 designed most of this: journeys J5–J8 and J10, boards B3–B7 and F1–F3, the escalation rule ("3 consecutive proxy-level failures, or one auth/TLS failure", docs/m1/04 §4), backoff of 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 and 30 s with jitter (J6), a 24-hour retention (docs/m1/06 §7) and the filters All, Errors, Connection and Blocked (J10). M5 and M6 left what M7 builds on: the session's serial event loop and its rule that once connected a failure never removes the VPN interface (ADR-0010); the engine's per-flow byte counters, its count of proxy-level failures in a row and its `ResetFlows` (M4); a `LogRepository` that held placeholder entries in memory; and no reaction to a proxy that stops answering while connected, nor to the phone's network changing.

## Decision

1. **One typed entry per event, nothing free-form.** A log entry has a time, a level (info, warning, error), an event (`connect_requested`, `stage_reached`, `connected`, `connect_failed`, `blocked`, `reconnect_started`, `reconnect_attempt_failed`, `reconnected`, `reconnect_gave_up`, `offline`, `network_changed`, `stopped`, `revoked`, `session_summary`, `connection_problems`), the stage it happened at, the catalogue code when something failed, the profile's random id (never its name) and details from a fixed list of keys (protocol, TLS to proxy on/off, sign-in yes/no, set-up time, attempt and limit, next wait, reason, counts, durations, bytes, the network's kind — "wifi", "cellular" — and the engine's fixed phrase for a failure). Every value goes through `LogSanitizer` on write, on read and on export: at most its first 1,000 characters are looked at (cut at a word's end; one word that long is redacted whole), control and format characters and line separators become spaces, and anything that looks like an IPv4 or IPv6 address (also a MAC address), a host or domain name (any script, with `_` or a leading `*.`), a name with a port (`myproxy:8080`), a URL, an e-mail address or `user:password@` (a password with spaces too), a sign-in's credentials — whatever follows `Basic` or `Bearer` but the few words a phrase has there ("HTTP Basic authentication"), a Digest, Negotiate or NTLM value with a digit or `+ / =`, and Digest's parameters (`username="…"`, realm, nonce, response) — or a long token is replaced, addresses and names also next to letters or dashes (`ip_203.0.113.7`, `--proxy.example.com`), then the value is cut to 160 characters; codes must look like catalogue codes. A bare one-word name (`squid`) can't be told from a word and is left as it is, as is a Digest, Negotiate or NTLM value of letters only (ProxyFlow signs in with Basic only). What the session writes is already counts, keys and fixed phrases — every fixed phrase of the engine's errors and of the session passes unchanged (`LogsTest` reads them from the sources and checks each); the sanitizer covers a phrase that came from the engine or a proxy. No destination is ever logged in M7, whatever a profile records for the engine.
2. **Stored in a database of its own.** `logs.db` (Room, `LogDatabase` version 1, schema exported and checked in CI like the profiles' one, ADR-0009), apart from the profiles: clearing it, its retention and its schema never touch a profile. It keeps at most 5,000 entries and none older than 24 hours: the bounds are applied in the same transaction as every write, when the app starts and every hour while it runs, and each time the Logs screen is shown; reading leaves out an entry past them even before the next purge, so none is ever shown. An entry dated more than an hour ahead of the clock (written while the clock was set ahead) is hidden and purged. Clear is counted, not timed: each one adds one to the log's generation, which every entry carries from when its event happened, so an entry of an event from before a Clear that arrives after it (a batch on its way, a minute's count) is dropped and cleared events never come back — and a clock set right after a Clear loses nothing written after it. Clear and every write take turns, so a batch that passed the check can't land after Clear's delete. SQLite's `secure_delete` overwrites deleted rows and the rollback journal is truncated after each write (no write-ahead log keeping old pages), so Clear leaves nothing of the entries readable in the file; both are set on the connection each time the database opens (Room's TRUNCATE only turns the write-ahead log off), and a device test reads them back from the app's own connection. The bounds are fixed in M7; settings for them wait for M9.
3. **Written off the session's thread.** `DiagnosticsRecorder` takes the session's entries without blocking it, writes them in batches of up to 100 on an I/O thread, drops the oldest unwritten ones past 1,000, and a write that fails only loses entries. Connections to sites that fail while the proxy works — as many as an app makes — are counted per profile and code and written once a minute as one `connection_problems` entry each, dated when the first of them failed, and when the session ends; a count from before a Clear goes with it, and the failures after it are counted afresh.
4. **What the session logs, and at which stage.** A request (with the protocol, TLS and sign-in, and whether it is a restart or a switch), each stage it reaches (B3's rows: starting, VPN permission, proxy handshake, authenticated), Connected with its set-up time, an attempt that ended with the phone back on its normal network with its stage and code (`connect_failed`: before the first connection nothing had changed; after it, the interface was lost — the VPN consent withdrawn during a switch or a reconnection, or the service destroyed), traffic held after a failure while connected (`blocked`, at the stage it happened), each reconnection (why, the failures in a row, each failed attempt with the next wait, the end: reconnected after how many attempts and how long without a connection — counted from when traffic was first held, through Offline and every attempt — or stopped after its last attempt; a profile first connected by an automatic attempt, after a switch to it failed, is logged as connected, its session starting then), no network, another network (and how many connections it ended), the disconnect (by the user, or because the profile was deleted), a revocation, and a summary when the session ends: its length, bytes up and down, connections opened and failed, UDP datagrams refused, pings dropped and reconnections. The stages an automatic attempt goes through are not logged one by one; its result is. Android refusing the foreground service fails the attempt at the VPN permission stage, before any proxy is contacted.
5. **The session's time and traffic.** A session lasts from the first Connected to a disconnect or a switch to another profile; reconnections, restarts and time blocked inside it are part of it. `connectedSinceMillis` is the session's start while Connected, Reconnecting or Offline; Blocked and a restart's set-up show none (Home shows the session's time only while connected), and Connected brings the same start back. Its traffic is what the engine relayed for apps' TCP connections through the proxy (bytes up and down, the payload of the apps' connections: no TCP/IP headers, no proxy handshake, no TLS to the proxy, no DNS answered on the phone, no pre-flight check), with the connections open now. An engine's counters are read just before it stops and added to the session's, in one step with Home's reads, so the counters never go back; an engine whose packet loop failed keeps its counters until it is stopped, so they are carried too; the next engine starts from zero. Home reads them once a second while it is shown. They are "approximate" in two ways the UI and this record state: protocol overhead is left out, and other apps' traffic through the VPN at the same time is counted too (it is the session's). The device check's tolerance follows from what is controlled: each controlled transfer's own connection, as the engine counts it, must carry exactly the bytes the app sent and received (tolerance 0 — no header, no retransmission, no handshake counted); the session's counters must grow by at least what the transfers carried, and what they read more is other apps' traffic, which the evidence reports for each run but can't bound (an emulator's Google Play services may download megabytes meanwhile).
6. **Reconnecting on its own.** While connected, a connection that fails at the proxy itself (a proxy-level code, `IsProxyLevel` in the engine) is checked against the escalation rule: a refused sign-in or certificate (`AUTH_REQUIRED`, `AUTH_FAILED`, `TLS_UNTRUSTED`, `TLS_HOSTNAME`, `TLS_EXPIRED`) blocks at once — no retry changes the answer (B6a, B6c); any other proxy-level failure makes the session reconnect once the engine has counted three in a row (a working connection resets its count). A SOCKS5 proxy that answered the method selection (and the sign-in) but not the connection request within the handshake timeout is still trying to reach the site — Dante waits for its own connect timeout before it answers — so that is the site's failure (`TARGET_UNREACHABLE`), never counted — when the deadline that passed was the handshake's own: a caller's earlier one (a connection's 15 s through the engine, a check's) is the caller's timeout (`HANDSHAKE_TIMEOUT`), whichever of the connection and the caller notices first. An HTTP proxy says nothing until it has reached the site, so its silence can't be told from a proxy that hangs and stays a proxy timeout (`HANDSHAKE_TIMEOUT`): three unresponsive sites in a row with no working connection between them reconnect, and the attempt passes at once if the proxy answers its check. Reconnecting stops the engine and holds traffic at the interface; each attempt is the pre-flight check (ADR-0010 §4) and, when it passes, a new engine on the same interface — what apps sent meanwhile is dropped, their connections retry and go through the new engine. The waits are 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 30, 30 and 30 s, each moved by up to ±20 % (jitter, so phones that lost the same proxy don't come back at once): eight attempts, about two minutes. An attempt that fails with a code worth another (`PROXY_UNREACHABLE`, `PROXY_DNS_FAILED`, `HANDSHAKE_TIMEOUT`, `TLS_HANDSHAKE`, `PROTOCOL_ERROR`, `TARGET_UNREACHABLE`, `TARGET_REFUSED`, `PROXY_GENERAL_FAILURE`, `INTERNAL`) waits for the next; any other code, or the last attempt failing, blocks with traffic held ("reconnecting stopped after 8 attempts"), and the user tries again or stops. Only one reconnection runs: every transition — disconnect, Stop, a switch, a restart, revocation, the service ending, a network change — cancels the waiting or running attempt first, a token makes a late wake-up of a cancelled wait do nothing, and "Retry now" only runs the waiting attempt sooner, with a new token, so the wait's own wake-up, should its timer have fired already, does nothing (ignored while an attempt runs; never two at once). Connecting and disconnecting quickly queues several starts of the VPN service: a start that reached it before the session let it go is not used, since the stop that followed ends the service unless a newer start keeps it, and that one attaches it again. The policy is a constructor parameter of the session and the app container; only the device tests pass another (shorter waits, through `TestRetry`).
7. **The phone's network.** `NetworkMonitor` registers a default-network callback once for the life of the process. ProxyFlow's own uid is outside its VPN (`addDisallowedApplication`), so its default network is the one the VPN's traffic leaves on, never the VPN (a VPN network is ignored all the same); the session gets its id and kind. Android's first report only says where the phone is. With no network while connected, reconnecting or held for a retry, the engine stops and the session waits **Offline** with traffic held (B7, `OFFLINE`); when a network is back it reconnects at once (attempt 1 without a wait). A move to another network while connected ends the engine's connections (`ResetFlows`, which also forgets the proxy's resolved addresses), so apps reconnect on the new network, and the session stays Connected; while reconnecting or held for a retry, the attempts start over at once. The VPN's underlying network stays "the default one" (`setUnderlyingNetworks(null)`), which Android follows by itself, and `setMetered(false)` makes the VPN as metered as that network.
8. **The screens.** Home: B3 shows one row per stage, done, in progress or not started; B4 adds what the session sent and received and the connections open now, marked approximate; B5, while reconnecting on its own, says why, which attempt of how many, and when the next one runs, with Retry now (disabled while an attempt runs), Edit profile, Stop and View logs; B7 says the phone is offline with traffic held; blocked and failed attempts offer the action that fixes the code first (Edit profile for a refused sign-in, a certificate, the protocol or the proxy's name; Try again otherwise; Android's date and time settings for a certificate that reads as expired), and the catalogue's variants for an HTTP proxy that offers only sign-in methods ProxyFlow lacks and one that allows no tunnels at all. The notification says "Attempt n of N" while reconnecting and "Waiting for a network" while offline. Logs: F1 says what will appear and what the log never holds; F2 lists entries newest first with their level in words, code and stage, filtered (All; Errors: warnings and errors; Connection; Blocked) and searched (codes, events, stages, details, profile names); an entry of another day than today shows its date too; F3 shows everything an entry holds and what to do about its code — for a site's code, whether it came from one connection while connected (other connections unaffected) or from the proxy check before connecting (the catalogue's pre-flight texts) — with Copy for that entry; Copy and Export hand over the entries shown as text with stable keys, sanitized again, built off the main thread, at most the newest that fit in 100,000 characters (Android passes the text on inside the intent and again as its clip data, through Binder transactions that share about a megabyte), after a header with the app's and Android's versions, the time, the filter and whether a search was applied — never the search's text, which may be a profile's name; a hand-over Android refuses says so instead of failing; Clear asks first. The sheet's rows read out as one, list rows say what a tap does, and before Android 13 copying an entry closes the sheet so the confirmation shows. An open sheet or Clear confirmation stays open when the screen is built again (a rotation, the dark theme, another language) and closes when the screen is left.
9. **Logcat.** The VPN lifecycle log (ADR-0010) adds the reconnections, the network's kind and the network changes, still without credentials, server names or anything apps send; the M6 evidence's parsing of "connect" and "connected" lines is unaffected (recovery logs "reconnected"). A Lint check of ProxyFlow's own, `LogSecret` (module `lint-rules`, applied by `:app:lintDebug` to every module the app uses), makes this a build rule, as the threat model's §6 item 3 asks: a logcat statement (`android.util.Log`, `VpnLog.add`, the session's `log`, `println`) that passes a value named like a credential, a server or a destination, bytes or characters, the engine's secret, credentials read from storage, a profile being edited, a whole profile or any of its fields but its id and protocol, or an exception's message or stack trace fails CI.

## Alternatives considered

- **Recover without restarting the engine** (J6 and the M1 lab check E10): the engine has no paused mode. Left running while the proxy is away, it accepts apps' connections and resets each one as its dial fails, so apps see errors and retry at once instead of waiting, and its failure count keeps growing. Stopping it holds apps' packets at the interface, the pre-flight check proves the proxy back before anything is sent, and a restart takes milliseconds; the session's counters carry over. This is a deliberate change from J6's acceptance line.
- **Block at once on `TLS_HANDSHAKE` and `PROTOCOL_ERROR` too** (the letter of the escalation rule, "one auth/TLS failure"): a connection reset in the middle of a handshake reads as one of these, so one would end a working session over a dropped packet. They count towards three in a row instead, and an attempt that fails with them is retried; a proxy that really stopped speaking TLS or its protocol is blocked after the last attempt. The certificate and sign-in refusals, which no retry changes, still block at once.
- **Retry for as long as traffic is held**: a user who doesn't look at the phone would keep a dead session for hours. The attempts are bounded; a network coming back or changing starts them again.
- **WorkManager for the retention purge**: the log only grows while the app runs, so applying the bounds on every write, at start, hourly and when the screen opens keeps it within them without a scheduled job.
- **Logs in the profiles' database**: one database would tie the log's schema, retention and clearing to the profiles', and `secure_delete` and the journal mode to both.
- **One entry per failed connection to a site**: an app retrying a refused site can fail hundreds of times a minute, which would push the session's own entries out of a bounded log; they are counted per code and minute.
- **Register the network callback only while connected**: the session must know which network it started on. A registration per connection races the state that triggers it (a quick disconnect and connect is never seen by the watcher), and a lost first report would make the next real change look like the first.
- **Counters in the notification**: Android limits a package's notification updates, and the notification's job is the state; Home shows them.

## Consequences

- Behaviour changes from M6, deliberately: a proxy that stops answering while connected no longer leaves the session Connected with every connection failing; after three failures in a row ProxyFlow reconnects, traffic held, and blocks if the proxy stays away. A certificate that changes while connected blocks traffic at once (B6c) instead of failing connection by connection, and Try again connects once the valid certificate is back. The M6 device tests and docs follow.
- While reconnecting or offline, nothing runs on the phone's side of the interface: DNS queries are held like everything else, so names are answered on the phone only while connected.
- The counters include other apps' traffic through the VPN and leave out protocol overhead; they are labelled approximate.
- A real switch between Wi-Fi and mobile data is not exercised on the CI emulators (switching their uplink mid-run is what scripts/e2e_device.sh avoids); the device tests tell the session of network changes themselves, and check the monitor's report against Android's own on each API level. A physical-device check belongs to M10.
- Export hands the user sanitized text they may share: times, codes, stages, counts, the network's kind and the first eight characters of the profile's random id, never a name, server, address or credential.
- Settings for the log (retention, a level, the option to record destinations M1 reserved) wait for M9.
- The catalogue's secondary actions "Learn more" (`TLS_UNTRUSTED`) and "Copy diagnostics" (`ENGINE_STOPPED`) are not on Home in M7: each blocked or failed state has View logs, whose Copy and Export hand over the diagnostics; the help pages "Learn more" opens are M9's.
- With an HTTP proxy, sites that don't answer within 10 s count as proxy timeouts (§6); on an idle phone whose apps keep trying such a site, ProxyFlow may reconnect now and then, which passes at once while the proxy answers.
- The other side of §6 for SOCKS5: a proxy that answers its greeting and sign-in but then never a connection request (its own way out broken, its exits used up) never makes ProxyFlow reconnect. The session stays Connected while every new connection fails after 10 s as the site's; the log counts them each minute (`connection_problems`, `TARGET_UNREACHABLE`) and Home shows them as connections to names the proxy couldn't find or reach, which points at DNS rather than at the proxy. An open item for M8: escalate on connection requests left unanswered several times in a row with no working connection between them, counted apart from the sites' refusals.
