## Acceptance criteria

| Criterion | Evidence | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Logs identify the failure stage without secrets or payloads | Every attempt the user starts logs each stage it reaches and, when it fails, that stage and the catalogue code; an automatic attempt logs its result (at stage reconnecting, with the code): `ReconnectTest.aConnectionLogsEachStageInOrderAndNothingSecret` (request, starting, VPN permission, proxy handshake, authenticated, connected; neither the server's name, the username nor the password in any entry) and `aFailedCheckLogsItsStageAndCode` (`AUTH_FAILED` at the proxy handshake, level error). On the emulators, `LogsEndToEndTest.aRefusedSignInIsLoggedWithItsStageAndNothingSecret` connects with a wrong password: the log has `connect_failed` at `proxy_handshake` with `AUTH_FAILED` and the stages before it, and none of the wrong password, the run's SOCKS username and password, the proxy's address and the profile's name is in the log's database file (its bytes, not what reading gives back), in the entries, or in the screen's own Export of them — searched by the profile's name, which the export only says was applied; `aSessionIsLoggedFromItsRequestToItsSummary` logs a session from its request through its stages, Connected with its set-up time and the disconnect to its summary (length, bytes, connections); `TlsEndToEndTest.aCertificateThatChangesWhileConnectedBlocksAtOnce` logs the block at stage connected with `TLS_UNTRUSTED`. ## Reviews and what they changed

Three independent reviews read the work, each told to find defects rather than confirm: the session and VPN layer; the log's storage, the screens and privacy; and the device tests with their evidence. Two more reviewers then read the fixes in turn, each the previous round's. Every finding was fixed or, where noted, documented as a limitation. The first CI run of the work (run 41574248857) had failed too; its failures follow the first three reviews.

## Decisions, deviations and open items

- **ADR-0012 accepted under the owner's delegation** ("Timi nai decide gara"): one typed entry per event, sanitized everywhere; the log in its own database with fixed bounds; the session's time and traffic carried across engine restarts; the escalation rule and the retry policy; reconnecting by restarting the engine on the same interface; the network monitor; the screens; the `LogSecret` build rule. It can be reopened.
- **Owner decisions still open:** D2 (plaintext proxies in the Play build) and D7 (TLS default in the form), both ADR-0005; D6 (what "Test connection" contacts, and the pre-flight target the privacy policy must name). None blocks M8.
- **A deviation from J6 and the M1 lab check E10, deliberate:** recovery stops the engine and restarts it on the same interface instead of keeping it running, because the engine has no paused mode and, left running, would reset every app's connection while the proxy is away; traffic stays held at the interface meanwhile, and the session's time and counters carry over (ADR-0012, Alternatives).
- **The counters include other apps' traffic.** What Home shows is everything apps sent and received through the proxy, labelled approximate. The tolerance that can be checked is the transfer's own connection's, which is exact; the session's excess over the transfers is reported for each run, not bounded: in the verified commit's run, up to 20.5 MB more came down during the HTTP transfers on API 35.
- **Unresponsive sites and proxies that hang.** A SOCKS5 proxy's late reply to a connection request is the site's failure (an engine change in this milestone), when the handshake's own 10 s are what passed; an HTTP proxy says nothing until it has reached the site, so 10 s of silence can't be told from a hanging proxy and counts as a proxy timeout — three unresponsive sites in a row with nothing working between them make ProxyFlow reconnect, which passes at once while the proxy answers its check. The other side, for SOCKS5: a proxy that answers its sign-in but then never a connection request (its own way out broken) never makes ProxyFlow reconnect; the session stays Connected with every connection failing, counted in the log each minute and on Home as names the proxy couldn't find or reach (ADR-0012 §6, Consequences).
- **What is measured on emulators, not phones.** A real switch between Wi-Fi and mobile data is not exercised on the CI emulators (their uplink is kept to one network, `scripts/e2e_device.sh`): the device tests tell the session of network changes through the call the network monitor makes, and check the monitor's report against Android's own on each level. Physical phones and vendor builds come with M10.
- **Not in M7, by plan:** settings for the log (retention, a level, the option to record destinations M1 reserved) and the help pages the catalogue's "Learn more" opens (M9). The catalogue's "Copy diagnostics" for `ENGINE_STOPPED` is served by View logs, whose Copy and Export hand over the diagnostics.
- **The sanitizer is a backstop.** Everything ProxyFlow writes is counts, keys and fixed phrases; the sanitizer covers a phrase from the engine or a proxy. A bare one-word name (`squid`) can't be told from a word and passes, as does a Digest, Negotiate or NTLM value of letters only or under 8 characters, and a Basic or Bearer one under 4 (ProxyFlow signs in with Basic only, whose values are longer); no phrase ProxyFlow writes holds one — `LogsTest` reads every literal phrase of the engine's errors and the session from their sources and checks that each passes unchanged. The rules prefer to take too much: a word after "basic" or "bearer" outside their short list, or the text from a word with a colon to an `@` up to 64 characters on, is redacted too (ADR-0012 §1).
- **While reconnecting or offline nothing is answered on the phone**, DNS included: the engine is stopped, so apps' queries are held like the rest of their traffic and retried.
- **The CI harness on API 34.** Airplane mode often leaves the API 34 image's mobile data up (`d71f55a`'s attempt 2, and attempts 1 and 2 of the verified commit's run). Wi-Fi then comes up only after the harness's check, so the uplink line names mobile data while saying the radio is off; the tests ran over Wi-Fi each time (the phone's Wi-Fi capture saw the app's connections to both proxies, the emulator's mobile-data capture none of them, and neither anything to the lab's destinations). Checking that the radio really is off before choosing, and wording that line from what was found, is for the next change to the harness.
- **Three things for M8 (kill switch and hardening)**, besides those M6 handed over: the M6 evidence's checks of other apps' traffic around connecting and disconnecting (M6 report, Decisions), which M7 leaves as they were; the reconnection's held traffic as the in-app part of the kill switch, next to Android's Always-on VPN with "Block connections without VPN", which M8 guides; and escalating on a SOCKS5 proxy's connection requests left unanswered several times in a row with no working connection between them, counted apart from the sites' refusals (above).
